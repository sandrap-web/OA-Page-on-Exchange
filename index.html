1 · About the OA

What is the OA?

The Goodwill Opportunity Accelerator® (OA) is a step-by-step approach to pairing the support services people need with career training, to help them build a career that lasts.

Objectives

The OA is designed to accomplish four objectives.

🎯
Equip Job Seekers

Provide access to resources needed to obtain employment, advance careers, and experience economic mobility.

🤝
Engage Teams & Partners

Connect Goodwill team members and partners with best-in-class resources to enhance workforce development services.

🌐
Connect Communities

Collaborate with national partners to connect Goodwill members and their communities to resources and support.

📣
Advocate & Promote

Engage in advocacy and public relations to educate communities and promote the success of the OA model.

2 · Adoption Levels

Five Levels of OA Adoption

Organizations move from Prospective to Platinum at their own pace, and may be at different levels in different areas. Gold and Platinum are the adoption goals.

A
Prospective

Not yet using OA practices in this area.

B
Exploring

Leaders see the value. Early, informal steps, but no plan yet.

C
Progressing

Practices are being standardized and documented.

D
★ OA Gold

Core adoption. You deliver fundamental OA services with flexibility in tools and delivery methods.

E
★★ OA Platinum

Full adoption of OA practices at their most complete. Specific tools (such as Northstar, ART, or Skills to Succeed) are optional; the practices are what count.

Click a phase to see what each level looks like. For more detail, open "See all 5 adoption levels" on any tool card.

KSDAs 1 to 6

A. Prospective

  • Staff organized by grant
  • No career navigator training or OA champion
  • Referrals and employer contact are informal
  • No consistent data

B. Exploring

  • A few informal partners
  • Data is inconsistent

C. Progressing

  • Functional roles and shared entry-point forming
  • Some career navigators and OA Fellow enrolled in training
  • Partnerships being documented, LMI use starting, CMS in place

D. ★ Gold

  • Formal referral and employer partnerships
  • Participant data tracked in a case management system, with follow-up at 30, 60, 90, and 180 days after placement

E. ★★ Platinum

  • Full functional model
  • Ongoing staff development, two or more Fellows
  • Data-driven partnerships, and employers co-create training

  • Monthly data reporting to GII

Organizational Preparation

The tools, resources and training in the Organizational Preparation step of the Goodwill Opportunity Accelerator® supports mission teams as you train your staff, align on systems and processes, gain internal stakeholder buy-in, and build community and employer partnerships.

Org Prep · 1

Functionally Aligned Staffing Model

Staff are organized by the work they do, rather than by program or funding source. Each core OA function has a clear owner, so participants get the same quality of service no matter which door they come through.

Gold: Functional groups, one point of entry Platinum: Full functional model, skills-based hiring
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveStaff organized by program, grant, or population; each new grant hires its own team doing every function.
B · ExploringLeaders recognize the silo problem. Some shared steps exist, but staffing is still largely program-by-program.
C · ProgressingFunctional groups forming. A shared point of entry exists; some staff serve across funding streams; roles being defined.
D · ★ GoldStaff organized into functional groups. One common point of entry. Participants served by function regardless of funding stream; blended funding documented.
E · ★★ PlatinumFull functional model at fidelity. Specialization within a function only where volume warrants. Skills-based hiring and change management drive improvement.

Phase 1: Assess & Plan

The tools, resources and training in the Assess and Plan phase of the Goodwill Opportunity Accelerator® supports Goodwill career coaches and navigators as they recruit job seekers, conduct intake and assessments, create career and financial plans, and collect and report intake and enrollment data.

Phase 1 · 7

Outreach & Recruitment

Promote mission services using diverse outreach methods and referral technologies. Outreach is planned around the participant's life and delivered with community organizations people already trust, so it reaches those least likely to come in on their own.

Gold: Encouraged, not required Platinum: Referral partners + referral technology
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveOutreach is passive; the program serves only those who already know about it. No intentional strategy.
B · ExploringSome outreach through a single channel. Messaging is generic, not tailored to those facing the greatest barriers.
C · ProgressingA recruitment strategy is forming: a few community partners and channels used; the team tracks which bring people in.
D · ★ GoldAn intentional, multi-channel strategy reaches priority populations with tailored, asset-based messaging. (Encouraged, not required.)
E · ★★ PlatinumSystematic outreach through established referral partners and referral technology. Outreach and enrollment demographics tracked.

Phase 1 · 8

Registration & Intake

Conduct intake services to meet with potential clients/job seekers, learn more about who they are, and collect demographic, work and educational history, and other personal information that determines eligibility. Intake follows the same steps for everyone and is a welcoming, trauma-informed conversation rather than a form.

Gold: Short KPI list Platinum: Long KPI list
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveNo standardized intake.
B · ExploringA basic intake form exists but fields vary by staff and are not aligned to OA KPIs. Intake is paperwork, not relationship.
C · ProgressingA standard intake form captures most short-list KPIs. Data entered into the CMS, with some delays.
D · ★ GoldIntake aligned to the OA short KPI list; every participant completes a consistent, welcoming, trauma-informed intake that flows into the ICFP.
E · ★★ PlatinumIntake aligned to the long KPI list; comprehensive baseline integrates with the ICFP and reporting.

Phase 1 · 9a

Needs Assessment

Holistic evaluation across financial capability, health care, food security, housing stability, transportation, legal issues, family care (adult and child), and clothing needed for employment. Results are used to match supports to what each person actually needs.

Gold: Valid tools, results feed the ICFP Platinum: Re-assessment to track progress
See all 5 adoption levels
Shared progression for all Assessment Selection areas (9a to 9d).
A · ProspectiveAssessment is informal or skipped.
B · ExploringSome assessments are used, but tools vary by staff and their validity is unverified. A single test may stand in for the whole picture. Accommodations are handled case by case, and results are applied inconsistently.
C · ProgressingThe organization is standardizing valid, reliable tools across the assessment domains and starting to combine more than one source rather than a single score. Accommodations and language access are being built in, and results are beginning to feed the participant’s plan.
D · ★ GoldA whole-person assessment across the four domains (needs, basic education, career readiness and interest, digital skills) using valid, reliable tools administered by qualified staff. Fairness, accommodations, and language access are standard, and results feed each participant’s Individual Career and Financial Plan.
E · ★★ PlatinumEverything at Gold. Adverse-impact and validity are reviewed on an ongoing basis, participants are re-assessed to track progress, and results consistently drive individualized pathways and re-planning.

Phase 1 · 10

Orientation

Provide welcome materials and sessions explaining available services. Orientation shows participants the full path ahead, what to expect, and what support is there, so they start with confidence.

Gold: All participants complete before enrollment Platinum: Improved using participant feedback
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveNo orientation.
B · ExploringAn informal welcome happens for some participants, but it is inconsistent, undocumented, and not required before enrollment. What someone learns depends on who greets them.
C · ProgressingA standard orientation exists and most participants complete it, though not yet universally or before enrollment. It covers services, but does not yet give participants a full picture of the pathway ahead.
D · ★ GoldAll individuals complete a consistent orientation prior to enrollment. It explains available services, how the OA journey works, and mutual expectations, and leaves participants with a clear picture of the path ahead and confidence that it is achievable. Delivered group or individual, virtual or in-person.
E · ★★ PlatinumOrientation is treated as a persistence strategy and refined over time. Every participant leaves understanding the full pathway and what success looks like, with a warm bridge into the ICFP, and participant feedback on the orientation experience is collected and used to strengthen it.

Phase 1 · 11

Individual Career & Financial Plan (ICFP)

A personalized plan, built together with the participant, with SMART goals the participant sets for their career pathway, financial stability, and other self-sufficiency goals.

Gold: Built with a navigator using SMART goals Platinum: Living plan that drives every service
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveNo individual plan.
B · ExploringSome participants have goal notes or a partial plan, but plans are inconsistent, written by staff rather than developed with the participant, and rarely revisited after the first meeting.
C · ProgressingA standard ICFP is being adopted and most participants have a written plan developed with them. Goals are becoming more specific, though career and financial goals are not yet fully integrated and plans are not consistently used to guide services.
D · ★ GoldEvery participant develops a written ICFP collaboratively with their Career Navigator, using SMART goals across both career pathway and financial stability. The plan identifies goals, barriers, and the combination of services that will achieve them, and services connect back to the plan.
E · ★★ PlatinumThe ICFP is a living strategy, reviewed and updated as participants progress, hit obstacles, or reach goals. Progress is tracked and shared with the participant so they can see movement, financial capability support is actively delivered alongside career services, and the plan drives every service decision.

Phase 2: Equip & Connect

The tools, resources and training in the Equip and Connect phase of the Goodwill Opportunity Accelerator® supports Goodwill career coaches and navigators with implementing skills training programs, providing holistic support services for program participants, and preparing job seekers for employment in the community.

Phase 2 · 12

Wraparound Supports

Provide support services matched to each person's assessed needs, including remedial education, financial capability, health care, mental health, food security, housing stability, transportation, legal issues, family care (adult and child), and clothing needed for employment. Staff introduce participants to partners directly rather than handing out a list.

Gold: Matched to assessed needs Platinum: Proactive, whole-person support
See all 5 adoption levels
A · ProspectiveBarriers go unaddressed.
B · ExploringSome supports offered reactively and inconsistently, not tied to assessed needs, often only when a participant asks.
C · ProgressingA defined menu of supports exists and is offered to many participants, though not consistently matched to assessed barriers.
D · ★ GoldWraparound supports offered aligned to each participant’s assessed needs, delivered directly or through partners, connected to ICFP barriers. Utilization tracked.
E · ★★ PlatinumProactive, individualized support: barriers anticipated, emergency assistance prevents disruption, and a whole-person, two-generation lens is applied.
